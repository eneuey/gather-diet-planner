What do those letters mean?

RDA meets the needs of nearly all healthy people. AI is used when evidence isn't enough to set an RDA. EAR meets the needs of about half. AMDR is a healthy range for energy-giving nutrients.

△

A ceiling, not a target

The UL is the highest usual intake unlikely to pose risk. It is not a goal, and includes food, drinks, and supplements where applicable. More isn't always better.

✳

Keep it flexible

Food planning can be imperfect and still be useful. Think patterns over time, enjoy variety, and make changes that feel realistic for you.

✳

Your wellbeing comes first.

This tool cannot assess medical needs or determine whether a plan is complete or safe. If you have a medical condition, a history of disordered eating, or pregnancy-related concerns, seek individualized guidance from a qualified clinician or registered dietitian.

✳
TRANSPARENCY MATTERS

Where these numbers come from

Reference values: National Academies Dietary Reference Intakes, summarized by the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Adult values shown are for ages 19–50 and healthy populations. Some nutrients vary by age, reference category, and life stage. Unavailable food values are left blank.

Food estimates: USDA FoodData Central, Food and Nutrient Database for Dietary Studies (FNDDS) 2021–2023. Catalog values are per 100 g; serving weights and descriptions are from USDA household portions. Individual recipes and foods vary.
